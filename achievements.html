Why Track Your Mathematics Progress?

Research-backed reasons for monitoring your own learning

Tracking your mathematics progress is not merely a motivational tool — it is a fundamental part of the learning process itself. Research in educational psychology consistently shows that students who monitor their own performance improve significantly faster than those who do not. When you can see exactly how many problems you have solved, what percentage you answered correctly, and which specific topics still need work, you gain the ability to study more strategically and efficiently.

The Competency Based Curriculum (CBC) introduced by the Kenya Institute of Curriculum Development (KICD) places particular emphasis on self-regulated learning — the ability of students to plan, monitor, and reflect on their own academic development. This achievements system is designed to support exactly that CBC competency. By reviewing your accuracy score and topic mastery bars regularly, you practise the same reflective learning skills that the TSC and MoE frameworks identify as essential for 21st-century mathematical proficiency.

For students preparing for the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE), progress tracking provides an early warning system. If your Algebra accuracy is high but your Trigonometry bar is still at 20%, that is a clear signal to spend more time on the Sine Rule, unit circle, and trigonometric identities before the examination. Effective KCSE preparation is built on this kind of honest, data-driven self-assessment — not on guessing which topics to study.

All data on this page is stored exclusively in your browser's local storage. No personal information is ever sent to a server. Your progress belongs entirely to you, is visible only on your device, and can be reset at any time using the button at the bottom of this page.

Study Tips for Earning Badges Faster

Practical strategies for Kenyan CBC mathematics students

Earning badges is a rewarding way to stay motivated, but the real benefit is the mathematical mastery you build along the way. The following strategies are based on how CBC's competency-based approach recommends students engage with mathematics — actively, reflectively, and consistently.

📅 Practise Daily for Streaks

The 3-Day and 7-Day streak badges reward consistent daily practice. Even solving just 5 problems per day builds stronger long-term retention than cramming 35 problems in a single session. Spaced repetition is the most research-validated learning strategy in mathematics education.

🎯 Focus on Accuracy, Not Speed

The Accuracy Star, High Achiever, and Precision Master badges reward correctness above volume. Work through each step methodically, check your arithmetic, and re-read the question before submitting. A 90% accuracy rate on 50 problems is far more valuable than 50% accuracy on 200 problems.

📚 Cover All 13 Topics for the Top Badge

The Well-Rounded Scholar badge — the hardest gold badge — requires recorded progress in all 13 CBC topics. This mirrors what KCSE Paper 1 and Paper 2 test: broad curriculum coverage, not depth in only one or two areas. Use the topic mastery bars to identify which topics still show 0% and prioritise those.

🧠 Use Brain Games for Fluency

The Game Champion badge unlocks after winning 5 brain games. These games build mathematical fluency — the ability to recall and apply mathematical facts quickly and accurately. Fluency is a core CBC competency and reduces the cognitive load during examinations, freeing mental capacity for complex problem-solving.

📖 Read the Lesson Before Practising

Attempting practice problems cold — without reading the lesson first — produces low accuracy and slow badge progression. Each topic lesson on this site is written specifically to prepare you for the practice problems. Read the worked examples, understand the method, then attempt the problems. This is exactly the learning sequence recommended in the CBC BECF framework.

❓ Ask Mwalimu When Stuck

Low accuracy in a topic often indicates a conceptual gap — a step in the reasoning that is not yet understood. Rather than guessing repeatedly, use Ask Mwalimu to get a step-by-step explanation of exactly where your understanding broke down. Targeted explanation is always more efficient than repeated guessing.
