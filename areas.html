Section 1 · Foundation

Basic 2D Area Formulas

Junior Secondary

The area of a shape is the amount of surface it covers, measured in square units such as cm², m² or km². In the CBC Junior Secondary curriculum (Grade 7–9), learners develop the concept of area by counting unit squares, then derive formulas for common 2D shapes. Understanding area is essential in real-life contexts — from finding how much paint a wall needs to calculating the size of a farm.

Standard Area Formulas

Each formula below is derived from first principles. Understanding the derivation helps you reconstruct the formula even if you forget it during an exam.

Shape
Formula & Key Variables
Rectangle
A = l × w length × width
Square
A = s² s = side length
Triangle
A = ½ × b × h b = base, h = perpendicular height
Parallelogram
A = b × h h = perpendicular height, NOT the slant side
Rhombus
A = ½ × d₁ × d₂ d₁, d₂ = diagonals
Trapezium
A = ½(a + b) × h a, b = parallel sides; h = perpendicular height
Kite
A = ½ × d₁ × d₂ same as rhombus — diagonals are perpendicular
💡
Why does A = ½bh for a triangle? Any triangle can be enclosed in a rectangle of the same base and height. The triangle occupies exactly half that rectangle's area — which is why every triangle formula has the ½ factor.

Worked Examples — Basic Shapes

Worked Examples
Ex 1 Rectangle: length = 12 cm, width = 7 cm. Find the area.
State
A = l × w
Sub
A = 12 × 7
Units
Both in cm, so area in cm²
✓ A = 84 cm²
Ex 2 Trapezium: parallel sides 9 cm and 15 cm, perpendicular height 6 cm.
Formula
A = ½(a + b)h
Sub
A = ½ × (9 + 15) × 6
Simplify
A = ½ × 24 × 6 = ½ × 144
✓ A = 72 cm²
Ex 3 A triangular farm has base 40 m and perpendicular height 25 m. Calculate its area in hectares. (1 ha = 10 000 m²)
Formula
A = ½ × b × h
Sub
A = ½ × 40 × 25 = 500 m²
Convert
500 ÷ 10 000 = 0.05 ha
✓ A = 500 m² = 0.05 hectares
Ex 4 A rhombus has diagonals of length 16 cm and 10 cm. Find its area.
Formula
A = ½ × d₁ × d₂
Sub
A = ½ × 16 × 10 = ½ × 160
✓ A = 80 cm²
⚠️
Common KCSE trap: For a parallelogram or triangle, always use the perpendicular height — not the slant side length. If only the slant side is given, you must first find the perpendicular height using Pythagoras' theorem or trigonometry.

Section 2 · Secondary

Circles, Sectors & Segments

Grade 9–11

Circular areas appear throughout the CBC curriculum, from the area of a round garden plot at Junior Secondary level to arc-length problems and segment areas in Senior Secondary. The constant π (pi ≈ 3.14159…) connects a circle's radius to its area and circumference. When angles are expressed in radians — the preferred unit at A-Level and university — the sector and segment formulas become especially elegant.

Key Circle Formulas

Circle — Core Formulas
Area of full circle A = πr²
Circumference C = 2πr = πd
Sector area (degrees) A = (θ/360°) × πr²
Sector area (radians) A = ½r²θ
Arc length (degrees) l = (θ/360°) × 2πr
Arc length (radians) l = rθ
Segment area (radians) A = ½r²(θ − sin θ)
🧮
Derivation of segment area: A segment is the region between a chord and the arc it subtends. Segment area = Sector area − Triangle area = ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ = ½r²(θ − sin θ). The triangle area ½r² sin θ uses the formula A = ½ab sin C with a = b = r and C = θ.

Worked Examples — Circles & Sectors

Worked Examples
Ex 5 Find the area and circumference of a circle with radius 7 cm. (Use π = 22/7)
Area
A = πr² = (22/7) × 7² = (22/7) × 49 = 22 × 7 = 154 cm²
Circumference
C = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 2 × 22 = 44 cm
✓ A = 154 cm², C = 44 cm
Ex 6 A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 80°. Find its area and arc length. (π = 3.142)
Area
A = (80/360) × π × 9² = (2/9) × 3.142 × 81 = (2/9) × 254.502 ≈ 56.56 cm²
Arc length
l = (80/360) × 2π × 9 = (2/9) × 3.142 × 9 × 2 = (2/9) × 56.556 ≈ 12.57 cm
✓ Area ≈ 56.56 cm², Arc length ≈ 12.57 cm
Ex 7 A sector has radius 10 cm and angle π/3 radians. Find the segment area.
Segment formula
A = ½r²(θ − sin θ)
Values
r = 10, θ = π/3 ≈ 1.0472, sin(π/3) = √3/2 ≈ 0.8660
Calculate
A = ½ × 100 × (1.0472 − 0.8660) = 50 × 0.1812
✓ A ≈ 9.06 cm²
Ex 8 (KCSE-style) The area of a sector is 462 cm² and its radius is 21 cm. Find the angle of the sector in degrees. (π = 22/7)
Formula
A = (θ/360) × πr²
Rearrange
θ = (A × 360) / (πr²) = (462 × 360) / ((22/7) × 441)
Simplify
= 166 320 / (22 × 63) = 166 320 / 1 386
✓ θ = 120°

Section 3 · Secondary

Advanced 2D Shapes

Grade 10–12

Beyond the standard quadrilaterals and triangles, the CBC Senior Secondary curriculum introduces regular polygons, composite shapes, and coordinate-based methods for finding areas. These topics are examinable in KCSE Paper 2 and are prerequisites for integration-based area work at A-Level.

Regular Polygons

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all interior angles equal. The area of any regular polygon with n sides each of length s is given by:

Regular Polygon
A = (ns²) / (4 tan(π/n))

This formula comes from dividing the polygon into n congruent isosceles triangles from the centre, each with a base of s and a height of s/(2 tan(π/n)).

Areas Using Coordinates — Shoelace Formula

Given a polygon with vertices listed in order (x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂), …, (xₙ, yₙ), the Shoelace (or Gauss) formula gives the exact area:

Shoelace Formula
A = ½ |Σᵢ (xᵢ yᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁ yᵢ)| where the indices wrap around: xₙ₊₁ = x₁, yₙ₊₁ = y₁

Ellipse

Ellipse (semi-axes a and b)
A = πab

When a = b = r, this reduces to A = πr² — the circle formula. An ellipse with a = 5 cm and b = 3 cm has area = π × 5 × 3 = 15π ≈ 47.12 cm².

Worked Examples — Advanced 2D

Worked Examples
Ex 9 Find the area of a regular hexagon with side length 8 cm.
Formula
A = (ns²) / (4 tan(π/n)), where n = 6, s = 8
Sub
A = (6 × 64) / (4 × tan(π/6)) = 384 / (4 × (1/√3)) = 384 / (4/√3)
Simplify
A = 384 × √3/4 = 96√3 ≈ 96 × 1.7321
✓ A = 96√3 ≈ 166.28 cm²
Ex 10 A quadrilateral has vertices A(1, 1), B(5, 1), C(6, 4), D(2, 5). Find its area using the Shoelace formula.
List
(x₁,y₁)=(1,1), (x₂,y₂)=(5,1), (x₃,y₃)=(6,4), (x₄,y₄)=(2,5)
Forward
Σ(xᵢyᵢ₊₁) = (1×1)+(5×4)+(6×5)+(2×1) = 1+20+30+2 = 53
Backward
Σ(yᵢxᵢ₊₁) = (1×5)+(1×6)+(4×2)+(5×1) = 5+6+8+5 = 24
Area
A = ½|53 − 24| = ½ × 29
✓ A = 14.5 square units
Ex 11 A composite shape consists of a rectangle 10 cm × 6 cm with a semicircle on one of the shorter ends. Find the total area. (π = 3.142)
Rectangle
A₁ = 10 × 6 = 60 cm²
Semicircle
Radius = 6/2 = 3 cm. A₂ = ½πr² = ½ × 3.142 × 9 = 14.139 cm²
Total
A = 60 + 14.139
✓ A ≈ 74.14 cm²

Section 4 · Secondary

Surface Area of 3D Shapes

Grade 10–12

Surface area is the total area of all faces of a 3D solid. In the CBC curriculum, surface area is taught alongside volumes and is frequently examined in the context of real-world problems such as painting a storage tank, wrapping a gift, or manufacturing a container. The key is to identify every face and add their individual areas carefully.

Surface Area Formulas

3D Shape
Surface Area Formula
Cube (side a)
SA = 6a²
Cuboid
SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
Cylinder (r, h)
SA = 2πr(r + h) = 2πr² + 2πrh
Cone (r, slant l)
SA = πr(r + l) = πr² + πrl
Sphere (radius r)
SA = 4πr²
Hemisphere (radius r)
SA = 3πr² curved + flat circular base
Triangular prism
SA = 2(triangle area) + perimeter × length
📐
Slant height of a cone: If you are given the vertical height h and radius r, find the slant height using Pythagoras: l = √(r² + h²). KCSE questions often give the vertical height, so you must calculate l before substituting into the surface area formula.

Worked Examples — Surface Area

Worked Examples
Ex 12 Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. (π = 3.142)
Formula
SA = 2πr(r + h)
Sub
SA = 2 × 3.142 × 5 × (5 + 12) = 2 × 3.142 × 5 × 17
Calculate
SA = 2 × 3.142 × 85 = 534.14 cm²
✓ SA = 534.14 cm²
Ex 13 A cone has base radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Find its total surface area. (π = 3.142)
Slant height
l = √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 cm
SA formula
SA = πr(r + l) = 3.142 × 6 × (6 + 10)
Calculate
SA = 3.142 × 6 × 16 = 3.142 × 96
✓ SA = 301.63 cm²
Ex 14 Find the surface area of a sphere with diameter 14 cm. (π = 22/7)
Radius
r = 14/2 = 7 cm
SA
SA = 4πr² = 4 × (22/7) × 49 = 4 × 22 × 7 = 4 × 154
✓ SA = 616 cm²
Ex 15 (KCSE-style) A water tank is in the form of a closed cylinder of radius 1.4 m and height 2 m. Calculate the cost of painting its outer surface at Ksh 850 per m². (π = 22/7)
SA
SA = 2πr(r + h) = 2 × (22/7) × 1.4 × (1.4 + 2) = 2 × (22/7) × 1.4 × 3.4
Simplify
= 2 × 22 × 0.2 × 3.4 = 2 × 22 × 0.68 = 2 × 14.96 = 29.92 m²
Cost
Cost = 29.92 × 850 = Ksh 25 432
✓ Total cost = Ksh 25,432

Section 5 · A-Level

Areas Using Integration

A-Level

Integration gives us an exact method for finding the area under any continuous curve — not just the standard geometric shapes. This is a core topic in Kenya's A-Level Mathematics (KUCCPS pathway) and connects directly to the Calculus strand in the CBC Senior Secondary curriculum. The definite integral is the precise sum of infinitely many infinitely thin rectangles, a concept introduced rigorously by Riemann.

Fundamental Area Formulas

Integration-Based Areas
Area under curve (above x-axis) A = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx
Area below x-axis (curve negative) A = −∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = ∫ₐᵇ |f(x)| dx
Area between two curves A = ∫ₐᵇ [f(x) − g(x)] dx where f(x) ≥ g(x)
Area w.r.t. y-axis A = ∫_c^d x dy = ∫_c^d g(y) dy
Polar area A = ½ ∫_α^β [r(θ)]² dθ
⚠️
Sign warning: If a curve dips below the x-axis, the integral gives a negative value for that part. When the question asks for total area, you must split the integral at the x-intercepts and take the absolute value of each part before adding. Forgetting this is one of the most common errors in A-Level calculus.

Method: Finding Area Under a Curve

  1. Sketch the curve — identify whether it is above or below the x-axis on each interval.
  2. Find x-intercepts — set f(x) = 0 and solve; these are your limits of integration.
  3. Set up the integral — use definite integral from a to b.
  4. Integrate — find the antiderivative F(x) of f(x).
  5. Evaluate — compute F(b) − F(a).
  6. Check sign — if the value is negative, the region is below the x-axis; take the absolute value for the area.

Worked Examples — Integration

Worked Examples
Ex 16 Find the area under y = x² + 1 between x = 0 and x = 3.
Integrate
∫₀³ (x² + 1) dx = [x³/3 + x]₀³
Evaluate
= (27/3 + 3) − (0 + 0) = (9 + 3) − 0 = 12
✓ A = 12 square units
Ex 17 Find the area enclosed between y = x² and y = x + 2.
Intersections
x² = x + 2 → x² − x − 2 = 0 → (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0 → x = −1 or x = 2
Which is top?
At x = 0: top curve y = x + 2 gives 2; bottom y = x² gives 0. So (x + 2) ≥ x² on [−1, 2].
Integral
A = ∫₋₁² [(x + 2) − x²] dx = ∫₋₁² (x + 2 − x²) dx
Antiderivative
= [x²/2 + 2x − x³/3]₋₁²
Evaluate at 2
= (4/2 + 4 − 8/3) = (2 + 4 − 8/3) = 6 − 8/3 = 10/3
Evaluate at −1
= (1/2 − 2 + 1/3) = (1/2 + 1/3 − 2) = 5/6 − 2 = −7/6
Total
A = 10/3 − (−7/6) = 10/3 + 7/6 = 20/6 + 7/6 = 27/6
✓ A = 4½ square units
Ex 18 Find the total area between y = sin x and the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 2π.
Note
sin x ≥ 0 on [0, π] and sin x ≤ 0 on [π, 2π]. Must split.
Part 1
∫₀^π sin x dx = [−cos x]₀^π = (−cos π) − (−cos 0) = 1 + 1 = 2
Part 2
∫_π^{2π} sin x dx = [−cos x]_π^{2π} = (−1) − (1) = −2 → |−2| = 2
Total area
A = 2 + 2 = 4
✓ Total area = 4 square units
Ex 19 — Verification Verify the area of an ellipse x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 equals πab using integration.
Top half
y = (b/a)√(a² − x²), integrate from −a to a and double (or 0 to a and multiply by 4).
Integral
A = 4∫₀ᵃ (b/a)√(a² − x²) dx
Substitution
Let x = a sin t, dx = a cos t dt. When x = 0, t = 0; when x = a, t = π/2.
Simplify
A = 4(b/a) ∫₀^{π/2} a cos t · a cos t dt = 4ab ∫₀^{π/2} cos²t dt = 4ab · π/4
✓ A = πab ✓ Confirmed

Section 7 · Exam Guidance

Common Errors & How to Avoid Them

All Levels

These are the most frequently penalised errors in KCSE Mathematics marking. Study each carefully before your examination.

✗ Wrong Triangle with base 8, slant side 5: A = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm²
✓ Right Find perp. height h first: h = √(5²−4²) = 3. A = ½ × 8 × 3 = 12 cm²
Always use perpendicular height, never the slant side, in area formulas.
✗ Wrong Circle diameter = 10 cm → A = π × 10² = 100π cm²
✓ Right r = d/2 = 5 cm → A = π × 5² = 25π cm²
Always halve the diameter to get the radius before squaring.
✗ Wrong Area between y=x² and y=x: using wrong limits
✓ Right Always find intersection points first (solve f(x)=g(x)) to get exact limits a and b.
Incorrect limits of integration are the #1 error in calculus area problems.
✗ Wrong Cone SA = πrl only (forgetting the base)
✓ Right Total SA = πrl + πr² = πr(l + r). Only omit base if the question says "curved surface area".
Read the question: "total" SA includes all faces; "curved" or "lateral" SA does not.
✗ Wrong Sector angle 120° in radian formula: A = ½r²×120
✓ Right Convert first: 120° = 120×π/180 = 2π/3 rad. Then A = ½r²×(2π/3).
The radian formula A = ½r²θ requires θ in radians, not degrees.
✗ Wrong ∫₀² (x²−4) dx → treating result as area directly
✓ Right Since x²−4 < 0 on [0,2], area = |∫₀²(x²−4)dx| = |[x³/3−4x]₀²| = |8/3−8| = 16/3.
When the curve is below the x-axis, take the absolute value of the definite integral.
