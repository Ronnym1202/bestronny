📝 Worked Sample Problems — Exam-Style 6 examples

Study these fully worked examples before using the generator. Each is presented in the exact format used in KCSE and CBC Senior Secondary assessments — a problem, then a complete numbered solution with the rule or method stated first.

Differentiation

Find dy/dx for y = 3x⁴ − 5x² + 7x − 2

Power rule: d/dx(cxⁿ) = cnxⁿ⁻¹
  1. d/dx(3x⁴) = 12x³
  2. d/dx(−5x²) = −10x
  3. d/dx(7x) = 7, d/dx(−2) = 0
dy/dx = 12x³ − 10x + 7
Integration

Evaluate ∫(2x³ − 3x + 4) dx

∫cxⁿ dx = (c/n+1)xⁿ⁺¹ + C
  1. ∫2x³ dx = ½x⁴
  2. ∫−3x dx = −(3/2)x²
  3. ∫4 dx = 4x — always add + C
= ½x⁴ − (3/2)x² + 4x + C
Matrices

Find det(A) for A = [[2, 3], [1, 5]]

det([[a,b],[c,d]]) = ad − bc
  1. a=2, b=3, c=1, d=5
  2. det = (2)(5) − (3)(1)
  3. = 10 − 3 = 7
det(A) = 7 (matrix is invertible)
Statistics

Find the mean and standard deviation of: 4, 7, 9, 6, 4

Mean = Σx/n | SD = √(Σ(x−x̄)²/n)
  1. Sum = 4+7+9+6+4 = 30, n = 5
  2. Mean = 30/5 = 6
  3. Deviations²: 4+1+9+0+4 = 18, Var = 18/5 = 3.6
Mean = 6, SD = √3.6 ≈ 1.897
Trigonometry

In △ABC, a = 5, b = 7, C = 60°. Find c.

Cosine Rule: c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos(C)
  1. c² = 25 + 49 − 2(5)(7)cos(60°)
  2. = 74 − 70(0.5) = 74 − 35 = 39
  3. c = √39 ≈ 6.245
c ≈ 6.245
Vectors

If a = (3, 4, 0), find |a| and â

|v| = √(x²+y²+z²) | û = v/|v|
  1. |a| = √(9 + 16 + 0) = √25 = 5
  2. â = (3/5, 4/5, 0)
  3. = (0.6, 0.8, 0)
|a| = 5, â = (0.6, 0.8, 0)

📖 How to Use the Practice All Levels

The practice is built on a single educational principle: active problem-solving produces deeper learning than passive reading. Every time you attempt a problem — even incorrectly — your brain engages with the material at a level that simply reading a textbook never achieves. This is called the testing effect, one of the most well-supported findings in cognitive science.

Click Generate a Problem and choose one of the 13 CBC-aligned topics. A fresh, randomised question appears — write it down and attempt it yourself before typing anything into the answer box. Once you have an answer, submit it: you will receive instant feedback and a complete step-by-step solution. Reading through the solution carefully — even when correct — is where the real learning happens.

For questions not covered by the generator, use the Ask Any Question box. Type your problem in plain English or standard mathematical notation and the built-in solver will work through it with full working. If still stuck, post it on the Ask Mwalimu board and receive a personal reply.

🎯 How to Get the Most From Practice Study Tips

Research in educational psychology consistently shows that four practices produce the best long-term retention and examination performance.

  1. 1

    Attempt First — Then Check

    Always work through the problem on paper before checking the solution. Even a wrong attempt forces your brain to engage deeply with the material. The moment of discovering why your answer was wrong is more memorable than any amount of passive reading.

  2. 2

    Read Every Step of the Solution

    When the solution appears, read each numbered step carefully — not just the final answer. Notice which rule or formula was applied at each step, and what common errors the solution avoids. This builds your mathematical intuition over time.

  3. 3

    Repeat the Same Topic 3–5 Times

    Generate 3–5 problems on the same topic before moving to the next one. Spaced repetition — returning to a topic after an interval — dramatically improves long-term recall.

  4. 4

    Track Your Progress and Target Weak Areas

    Your accuracy and topic history are saved automatically. Visit My Progress regularly to identify which topics have the lowest accuracy — those are the areas that need the most practice before your examination.

⚠ Common Errors to Avoid Exam Guidance

Based on years of teaching and reviewing KCSE marking schemes, these are the most frequently penalised mistakes in each topic area. Knowing them in advance saves marks.

d/dx — Differentiation

Forgetting to bring the power down before reducing the index. Also, treating the derivative of a constant as the constant itself rather than zero.

∫ — Integration

Omitting the constant of integration + C in indefinite integrals — this is an automatic mark deduction in virtually every KCSE marking scheme.

[ ] — Matrices

Reversing the determinant formula: it is always ad − bc, never ad + bc. Also, attempting to find the inverse of a singular matrix (det = 0).

→ — Vectors

Confusing the magnitude (a scalar value) with the vector itself. For two vectors to be perpendicular, their dot product must equal exactly zero.

△ — Trigonometry

Using degrees when the formula requires radians — particularly in sector area (A = ½r²θ) and arc length (L = rθ) formulas where θ must be in radians.

∑ — Sequences

Confusing the nth term formula with the sum formula. For a GP sum to infinity, always verify that |r| < 1 before applying S∞ = a/(1−r).

i — Complex Numbers

Forgetting that i² = −1 when expanding brackets. Also, giving only the modulus when the argument (angle) is also required in full polar form.

⬡ — Volumes

Omitting the factor of 1/3 in the cone and pyramid volume formulas. Always state units³ (cm³, m³) in the final answer — unit marks exist.
