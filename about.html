Teaching Philosophy

How Every Lesson Is Designed

The mathematics lessons on this site are not written like textbooks. They are written as a qualified teacher would explain something in a patient one-on-one session — with context, common mistakes flagged, multiple solution approaches shown, and honest acknowledgement of where students typically struggle. Mwalimu Ronny's design approach follows five principles:

1

Conceptual Before Procedural

Before any formula, explain why it works. A student who understands where the quadratic formula comes from will never forget it under exam pressure. This is the CBC principle of deep competency, not surface memorisation.

2

Multiple Representations

Every concept appears as a formula, a numerical worked example, and — where possible — a real-world context. This reflects the KICD requirement to apply competencies across contexts, not merely demonstrate procedures.

3

Errors Are Teaching Moments

Every topic includes a Common Errors section showing the wrong approach alongside the correct one. Understanding why something is wrong is often more instructive than seeing why it is right.

4

Scaffolded Difficulty

Each lesson progresses from Foundational through Secondary to University level. A Grade 10 student and a first-year university student can both use the same page productively — just different sections of it.

5

Exam-Authentic Format

All worked examples mirror how KNEC presents questions in KCSE — with "Hence", "Show that", multi-part questions, and marks allocated per step. Students should never be surprised by exam question format.
